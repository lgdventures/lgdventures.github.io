Chrome Back history after a restart

GuidePublished Nov 9, 2026 · Updated Nov 9, 2026

What happens to the Back button after Chrome restarts?

Quick answer: When Chrome restores tabs after a restart, it keeps only about the last 6 Back pages of each tab. Hours of reading behind a tab can shrink to a short list. BackTrail keeps up to 500 pages per tab in its side panel, across restarts, with no account.

What Chrome keeps (~6 Back pages)

Inside a normal browsing session, each tab has its own Back/Forward path: the pages you opened in that tab, in order. After you quit Chrome, install an update, or the browser crashes and then restores your tabs, that path is trimmed. Chrome brings the tab back, but only about the last 6 Back pages stay available for that tab.

The rest of the path is gone from the native Back control. The pages may still appear in Chrome's global History if you visited them recently, but that list is not the same as "where this tab has been" in order, with times, ready to reopen in context.

Crash, update, or quit: same trim

The cut happens whenever Chrome has to restore a session:

  • You quit and reopen with "Continue where you left off".
  • Chrome updates and relaunches your windows.
  • A crash or forced quit restores tabs on the next launch.

In each case the open tabs return, but the deep Back trail behind each one does not. Research tabs, long docs, and multi-step shopping flows are the ones that hurt most: you remember there was a useful page earlier in that tab, and the Back button no longer reaches it.

What BackTrail keeps in the side panel

BackTrail is a free Chrome and Edge extension that records each tab's trail (page address, title, and time) locally in your browser. Free keeps up to 500 Back pages per tab. It does not rebuild Chrome's native Back menu; it shows the path in its own side panel so you can click an earlier page (or open it in a new tab).

Useful views after a restart:

  • This tab: the full trail for the active tab, newest first, with times.
  • Sites: tabs you used since you opened the browser (or every open tab by site).
  • Review: tabs with more Back history than the browser keeps, so you can finish pending work before you quit.
  • Closed: tabs you closed in the last 7 days on Free (90 with Pro), with their history. Reopen brings the tab back with its trail.

History never leaves your browser. The extension has no account or analytics. See the privacy policy.

How to use it after a restart

  1. Install BackTrail from the Chrome Web Store or Edge Add-ons.
  2. Browse as usual. Each tab builds its own trail in the background.
  3. After a quit, update, or crash, reopen Chrome or Edge and click the BackTrail icon.
  4. In This tab, pick an earlier page from the list. Use Review if you want tabs with long trails first; use Closed if you closed a tab by mistake within the retention window.

FAQ

How many Back pages does Chrome keep after a restart?

About the last 6 per restored tab. That is why a long path feels gone even though the tab itself came back.

Does BackTrail rebuild Chrome's native Back menu?

No. It keeps the path in the BackTrail side panel. Chrome's own Back control stays as Chrome ships it.

What is the Free vs Pro limit?

Free: up to 500 pages per tab, Sites, Review, 60 one-click bulk closes, Closed 7 days. Pro: USD 15 one-time, Closed 90 days, bulk close beyond those 60, up to 3 browsers.
